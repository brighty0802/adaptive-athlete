# Four-Day Programme and Exercise Progression Rules

**Status:** Working programme v0.1  
**Related document:** `training-principles.md`

The programme is built around four repeatable sessions rather than fixed weekdays.

Football matches and training determine where these sessions sit in the week.

---

# Session A — Lower Strength + Power

**Target duration:** approximately 55–65 minutes  
**Primary purpose:** lower-body strength, force production and explosive quality

| Order | Exercise | Working sets | Rep range | Target RIR | Rest |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Broad jump or countermovement jump | 3 | 3 | — | 2–3 min |
| 2 | Back squat | 3 | 4–6 | 1–2 | 3–4 min |
| 3 | Romanian deadlift | 3 | 6–8 | 1–2 | 2–3 min |
| 4 | Weighted lunges | 2 | 6–8 / leg | 1–2 | 2–3 min |
| 5 | Standing calf raise | 2 | 8–12 | 1–2 | 90–120 s |

### Session notes

- Perform jumps while fresh.
- Squat is the primary strength lift.
- The RDL gives the programme a hip-hinge/posterior-chain movement.
- Lunges provide unilateral strength.
- Do not turn the session into a conditioning circuit.

---

# Session B — Upper Strength

**Target duration:** approximately 55–65 minutes  
**Primary purpose:** upper-body strength with balanced push/pull development

| Order | Exercise | Working sets | Rep range | Target RIR | Rest |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Bench press | 3 | 4–6 | 1–2 | 3 min |
| 2 | Weighted row | 3 | 6–8 | 1–2 | 2–3 min |
| 3 | Standing shoulder press | 3 | 5–8 | 1–2 | 2–3 min |
| 4 | Pull-ups | 3 | 5–8 | 1–2 | 2–3 min |
| 5 | Lateral raises | 2 | 12–20 | 1–2 | 60–90 s |

### Session notes

- Bench and row form the primary horizontal push/pull pairing.
- Standing shoulder press adds vertical pressing and trunk demand.
- Pull-ups provide vertical pulling.
- Lateral raises are low-cost accessory volume.

---

# Session C — Lower Volume + Robustness + Power

**Target duration:** approximately 50–60 minutes  
**Primary purpose:** secondary lower-body stimulus, hamstring/adductor/calf capacity and explosive quality

| Order | Exercise | Working sets | Rep range | Target | Rest |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 1 | Lateral bounds or broad jumps | 3 | 3–4 / side or total | Maximum quality | 2–3 min |
| 2 | Leg press | 3 | 8–10 | 1–2 RIR | 2–3 min |
| 3 | Leg extensions | 2 | 10–15 | 1–2 RIR | 90–120 s |
| 4 | Nordic hamstring curl | 2 | 4–6 | Controlled quality | 2–3 min |
| 5 | Copenhagen adduction | 2 | 20–30 s / side | Controlled | 60–90 s |
| 6 | Bent-knee soleus raise | 3 | 10–15 | 1–2 RIR | 60–90 s |

### Session notes

- This session is intentionally less dependent on maximal axial loading than Session A.
- Nordics are performed for quality; do not chase ugly failure repetitions.
- Copenhagen work should progress through leverage/control before excessive loading.
- This is the first lower-body session to reduce when football congestion becomes high.

---

# Session D — Upper Volume

**Target duration:** approximately 50–60 minutes  
**Primary purpose:** upper-body hypertrophy/strength support with relatively low football interference

| Order | Exercise | Working sets | Rep range | Target RIR | Rest |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | Incline free-weight bench press | 3 | 6–10 | 1–2 | 2–3 min |
| 2 | Chin-ups | 3 | 6–10 | 1–2 | 2–3 min |
| 3 | Lat pulldown | 2 | 8–12 | 1–2 | 90–120 s |
| 4 | Bodyweight dips | 2 | 6–10 | 1–2 | 2 min |
| 5 | Curls | 2 | 8–12 | 1–2 | 60–90 s |

### Session notes

- Once bodyweight dips become easy at the top of the range, they can become weighted dips.
- Chin-ups and pulldowns deliberately use different rep ranges.
- This session can usually be  retained even during heavier football weeks.

# Exercise-by-Exercise Progression Rules

## 1. Back Squat

**Prescription:** 3 × 4–6  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2  
**Load increase:** approximately +2.5 kg; +5 kg only when clearly appropriate

### Progression

```text
4/4/4 → keep load
5/5/4 → keep load
6/6/5 → keep load
6/6/6 @ ≥1 RIR → increase load
```

Do not increase if 6/6/6 required technical breakdown or 0 RIR grinding.

### Regression rule

If the first working set falls below 4 clean reps at the intended effort, the load is too heavy for the current prescription. Reduce approximately 5% for the remaining work and reassess next exposure.

---

## 2. Leg Press

**Prescription:** 3 × 8–10  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2  
**Load increase:** smallest sensible machine increment

### Progression

Increase once all three sets reach 10 reps with acceptable depth and ≥1 RIR.

Machine numbers are not comparable across different leg presses, so the application should store the machine/exercise variant where practical.

---

## 3. Weighted Lunges

**Prescription:** 2 × 6–8 per leg  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2  
**Load increase:** approximately +2–4 kg total, depending on equipment

### Progression

Increase once both legs complete both sets at 8 controlled reps.

Do not progress load if balance, stride length or knee/hip control deteriorates.

---

## 4. Leg Extensions

**Prescription:** 2 × 10–15  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2  
**Load increase:** smallest machine increment

### Progression

Reach 15/15 with controlled movement and ≥1 RIR, then increase.

If the machine increment is large, allow reps to fall back toward 10 rather than forcing the previous rep number.

---

## 5. Romanian Deadlift

**Prescription:** 3 × 6–8  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2  
**Load increase:** +2.5–5 kg

### Progression

Increase after 8/8/8 with controlled eccentric, stable trunk position and ≥1 RIR.

Hamstring tension and movement quality matter more than simply moving the bar farther.

---

## 6. Standing Shoulder Press

**Prescription:** 3 × 5–8  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2  
**Load increase:** +1–2.5 kg where possible

### Progression

```text
25 kg: 6/6/6 → keep
25 kg: 7/7/6 → keep
25 kg: 8/8/7 → keep
25 kg: 8/8/8 @ ≥1 RIR → increase
```

Because overhead press often progresses slowly, micro-loading is strongly preferred over forcing a large jump.

---

## 7. Lateral Raises

**Prescription:** 2 × 12–20  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2  
**Load increase:** smallest available increment

### Progression

Increase after 20/20 with controlled reps and minimal momentum.

If the next dumbbell jump is too large, continue adding quality reps or use smaller plates/cables rather than accepting poor form.

---

## 8. Bench Press

**Prescription:** 3 × 4–6  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2  
**Load increase:** +2.5 kg

### Progression

Increase after 6/6/6 at ≥1 RIR with consistent range of motion and technique.

If progress stalls, first attempt to gain reps or RIR at the same load rather than automatically adding sets.

---

## 9. Bodyweight Dips

**Prescription:** 2 × 6–10  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2

### Progression

1. Build to 10/10 with clean depth.
2. Once 10/10 is repeatable with ≥1–2 RIR, add approximately 2.5 kg.
3. Return to roughly 6–8 reps and rebuild.

If bodyweight itself is below the minimum range, use assistance rather than repeated failure attempts.

---

## 10. Incline Free-Weight Bench

**Prescription:** 3 × 6–10  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2  
**Load increase:** smallest practical dumbbell/barbell increment

### Progression

Increase after 10/10/10 with ≥1 RIR.

If using dumbbells and the available jump is large, it is acceptable to remain at the current weight longer and accumulate reps/RIR before moving up.

---

## 11. Pull-Ups

**Prescription:** 3 × 5–8  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2

### Progression

1. Build bodyweight pull-ups to 8/8/8.
2. Add approximately 2.5 kg.
3. Return toward the bottom of the range.
4. Repeat.

Only strict reps count toward progression.

---

## 12. Chin-Ups

**Prescription:** 3 × 6–10  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2

### Progression

Build to 10/10/10 at bodyweight, then add approximately 2.5 kg and return toward 6–8 reps.

Because chin-ups and pull-ups are trained on different sessions, progress them independently.

---

## 13. Lat Pulldown

**Prescription:** 2 × 8–12  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2  
**Load increase:** smallest machine increment

### Progression

Increase after 12/12 with controlled scapular movement and no excessive torso swing.

---

## 14. Weighted Rows

**Prescription:** 3 × 6–8  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2  
**Load increase:** smallest sensible increment, commonly +2–4 kg depending on implement

### Progression

Increase after 8/8/8 with repeatable torso position and full controlled range.

Do not count progressively more body English as strength progression.

---

## 15. Curls

**Prescription:** 2 × 8–12  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2  
**Load increase:** smallest available increment

### Progression

Increase after 12/12 with controlled reps.

An occasional final set at 0–1 RIR is acceptable when recovery is good, but it is not required.

---

## 16. Nordic Hamstring Curl

**Prescription:** 2 × 4–6  
**Target:** high-quality eccentric control rather than muscular failure

### Progression hierarchy

1. improve control through the eccentric;
2. increase usable range of motion;
3. reduce assistance;
4. reach 6 high-quality reps;
5. only then consider additional load or a harder variation.

Do not progress merely because the athlete can collapse faster for more reps.

---

## 17. Standing Calf Raise

**Prescription:** 2 × 8–12  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2

Increase after 12/12 with controlled stretch and full plantar flexion.

---

## 18. Bent-Knee Soleus Raise

**Prescription:** 3 × 10–15  
**Normal RIR:** 1–2

Increase after 15/15/15 with controlled range.

---

## 19. Copenhagen Adduction

**Prescription:** 2 × 20–30 seconds per side

### Progression hierarchy

1. improve position and control;
2. increase hold duration toward 30 s;
3. progress to a longer lever;
4. add external load only after the harder leverage is controlled.

Do not train this to uncontrolled failure.

---

## 20. Jumps / Bounds

**Prescription:** usually 3 × 3–4  
**Target:** maximal quality

### Progression

Progress through:

- better measured jump height/distance;
- better landing control;
- more demanding but appropriate variation;
- modest external load only when justified.

### Stop rule

If jump output or landing quality noticeably deteriorates, stop the power work rather than adding junk volume.

---

# Universal Next-Session Algorithm

For normal double-progression exercises:

```text
IF exercise_was_skipped:
    do_not_treat_as_failure

ELSE IF all_sets >= rep_max
AND RIR_requirement_met
AND technique_good:
    increase_load_next_time

ELSE IF performance_improved:
    keep_load

ELSE IF target_not_reached:
    keep_load

ELSE IF three_exposures_show_no_improvement:
    investigate_plateau
```

`performance_improved` can mean:

- more reps;
- same reps with more RIR;
- cleaner technique.

---

# Plateau Decision Tree

```text
No progress this session
        ↓
Was exercise genuinely attempted?
        ├── No → no progression judgement
        └── Yes
             ↓
One exposure?
        ├── Yes → repeat
        └── No
             ↓
Any improvement in reps/RIR/technique?
        ├── Yes → repeat load
        └── No
             ↓
Three stagnant exposures?
        ├── No → repeat
        └── Yes
             ↓
Are several exercises also declining?
        ├── Yes → investigate systemic / football fatigue
        │          → reduce volume temporarily
        └── No → exercise-specific action
                   → micro-load / small reset / rebuild
```

# Football Load Modifiers

The four sessions are not tied permanently to weekdays.

## Normal football week

Use all four sessions if recovery and schedule permit.

## Match within 24 hours

Do not perform a full demanding lower-body session.

Prefer:

- upper session;
- very short activation/power work;
- rest/recovery.

## Match within approximately 24–48 hours

If a lower session must be performed:

- use the **Reduced** version;
- reduce accessory volume;
- avoid hard eccentric fatigue;
- leave more RIR.

## Two-match / congested week

Lower sessions become **maintenance-biased**.

Example:

```text
Jumps: 2 × 3
Squat: 2 × 4
RDL: 2 × 6
Finish
```

Upper sessions can remain closer to normal.

The aim is to keep the strength signal without compromising football performance.

# Phase Adjustments

The exercise list can remain broadly stable across the 146 days while prescriptions change.

## Days 1–21

- 2–3 RIR
- conservative loads
- normal/moderate reps
- prioritise repeatable technique

## Days 22–70

Use the default programme shown above.

## Days 71–112

Primary lift emphasis becomes slightly heavier:

- squat: 3 × 3–5 or 4–6
- bench: 3 × 3–5 or 4–6
- shoulder press: 3 × 4–7
- RDL / rows: 3 × 5–8

Accessories remain mostly unchanged.

## Days 113–133

- retain meaningful load;
- reduce unnecessary lower-body accessory volume;
- maintain jump/power quality;
- avoid repeated grinders.

## Days 134–146

- reduce total working sets roughly 40–60%;
- keep some moderate/heavy exposures;
- finish the block fresh enough to test progress.

# Starting Load Rule

Do not guess starting loads from ego or historical PBs.

For each exercise during calibration:

1. choose a load expected to be manageable;
2. aim for the bottom-to-middle of the rep range;
3. stop with approximately 2–3 RIR;
4. adjust the next session based on the result.

Example:

```text
Shoulder press target: 3 × 5–8

If 25 kg produces:
7 / 7 / 6 @ 2 RIR
→ good starting load.

If it produces:
5 / 4 / 3 @ 0–1 RIR
→ too heavy.

If it produces:
8 / 8 / 8 @ 5 RIR
→ too light.
```

The first three weeks exist partly to give the application trustworthy baseline data.

---

# Key Principle for the Software

The app should never ask:

> "Did the weight increase this week?"

It should ask:

> "Did performance improve relative to the target?"

That distinction allows a simple rules engine to make sensible decisions without pretending that training adaptation requires artificial intelligence.
